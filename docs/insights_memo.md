# FMCG Multi-Country Analytics — Insights Memo

**Prepared by:** Abhijit Sengupta
**Date:** [today's date]
**Dataset:** FMCG Multi-Country Sales (2021–2023, 7 countries, 1.1M transactions)

This analysis examines 1.1M FMCG sales transactions across 7 European countries (2021-2023) to answer three questions: are promotions profitable, when does seasonal demand shift, and how much revenue is lost to stockouts?

All three findings point to the same underlying opportunity: better use of historical data for proactive planning. Promotions currently reduce margin by 47% (40%→21%) for only 1.6-2.3x volume uplift — discount depth should be reduced and reserved for slow-moving stock rather than applied broadly. Seasonal demand peaks 20% above trough in July and December, meaning inventory for high-rotation products should be increased ahead of these known peaks rather than reactively. Stockouts cost an estimated €14.6M over three years, concentrated in the largest markets — many of these are predictable given consistent ~3% stockout rates and should be addressed through better inventory planning for fast-moving SKUs.

## Finding 1: Promotional Effectiveness

Promotions cut margin nearly in half across all categories (40% → 21%) while
driving only 1.6x–2.3x volume uplift — a trade-off that isn't always worth it.

Dairy is the clearest example: promotional days drove more units sold (82.1 vs
50.2 on non-promo days), confirming that promotions do increase volume. However,
net revenue on promo days (€303.38) was _lower_ than on non-promo days (€435.62)
— the discount given away exceeded the value of the extra volume gained.

**Recommendation:** Set promotional discount depth based on category-specific
margin sensitivity rather than a uniform discount policy. Reserve deeper discounts
for slow-moving stock, where clearing inventory outweighs the margin cost, rather
than applying broad promotions across high-velocity categories like Dairy.

## Finding 2: Seasonal Demand Patterns

Seasonal demand peaks in summer (June–August) and December, running 20% above
the February low point.

Monthly revenue analysis shows July as the highest month (€14.2M) and December
as a close second (€14.2M), compared to February's trough (€11.8M) — a 20.3%
gap between peak and trough. The summer peak is driven by Beverages and Snacks,
consistent with increased outdoor activity and warmer weather. The December
peak reflects festive-season consumption across categories, particularly Dairy
and Snacks, as households spend more on gatherings and holidays.

**Recommendation:** Increase inventory for high-rotation products ahead of the
summer and December peaks to reduce stockout risk during these predictable
demand windows. Where promotional support is used during these periods, keep
discount depth shallow (5–10%) to lift volume further without repeating the
margin erosion seen in Finding 1.

## Finding 3: Stockout Revenue Impact

An estimated €14.6M in revenue was lost to stockouts over 2021–2023,
concentrated in the largest markets.

The stockout rate is remarkably consistent across all 7 countries (2.96%–3.09%),
indicating this is a systemic inventory planning issue rather than a market-specific
problem such as poor logistics in one country. However, because revenue impact
scales with transaction volume, Italy (€4.2M) and Spain (€3.2M) together account
for 51% of total estimated losses — simply due to their larger market size, not a
worse underlying stockout rate.

**Recommendation:** Since the stockout rate is uniform, root-cause fixes — improved
demand forecasting, safety stock policies, and supplier lead time management —
would benefit every market proportionally. For fastest revenue recovery, prioritize
Italy and Spain first, where fixing the same 3% stockout rate yields the largest
absolute financial return. This connects directly to Finding 2 — pre-positioning
inventory ahead of the July and December demand peaks would directly reduce
stockout incidents during the periods when they're most costly.

## Summary of Recommendations

1. **Recalibrate promotional discount depth** by category margin sensitivity —
   reserve deep discounts for slow-moving stock rather than applying broadly.
2. **Pre-position inventory ahead of seasonal peaks** (July, December) for
   high-rotation products to reduce stockout risk during predictable demand windows.
3. **Prioritize inventory planning fixes in Italy and Spain** first for maximum
   absolute revenue recovery, while applying systemic improvements (forecasting,
   safety stock, supplier lead times) across all markets.
